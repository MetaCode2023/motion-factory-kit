# Order #42: the Taco Bell epiphany (vertical Short, ~60 s)

A guy in a drive-thru at 11:47 PM. The speaker asks "anything else?" and in the four seconds before he answers,
he falls through his own mind, down to his neurons, out to the stars and back to the start of time. A honk from
the car behind snaps him back. He says "No. That's everything" and means it twice. His receipt says **ORDER #42**
(the answer to life, the universe and everything, for those who know). Last frame: one tiny star in his eye.

The joke is the gap between the scale of the thought and the scale of the moment, and that the whole trip fits
inside one minute on the dashboard clock.

## Hook options (frame 0 shows the line on screen)

1. **"Nobody tells you enlightenment happens in a Taco Bell drive-thru."** (recommended: says the whole premise
   in one breath, funny, and it promises a payoff)
2. "I saw the entire universe between 'number four' and 'anything else?'"
3. "I just wanted a number four. I got the meaning of life."

About 140 words. Facts: ~86 billion neurons (Azevedo et al., 2009); carbon and iron are made in stars, the
universe is 13.8 billion years old (Planck 2018), the Earth is 4.5 billion. On screen only, small, under line 11:
*"We are a way for the cosmos to know itself." Carl Sagan, Cosmos (1980).*

Why this shape: the cosmic part is a real idea, not word salad. Line 6-8 is the "no one is in charge" view of the
self (there's no single neuron that is you; "you" is the story 86 billion cells tell). Line 9-11 is the Sagan idea
that we're made of star stuff and are how the universe looks at itself. Beginners get both from one image each.

## Design system (rules every shot obeys)

**A. The Circle.** Everything is the same shape at every scale, and each scale cuts to the next through it: the
drive-thru speaker grille, his pupil in the rear-view mirror, a neuron's cell body, a star, a galaxy, the observable
universe, then back to the circle of light at the pickup window and the sticker on the bag. Every cut in the trip is
an iris through the circle we're already looking at.

**B. The dashboard clock.** `11:47` in green LED sits low in frame the whole time. During the trip its digits run
up through scales (86,000,000,000 neurons, 13.8 BILLION YRS). After the honk it reads `11:47` again: the whole
trip took no time.

**C. Type that behaves.** The mundane world talks in dot-matrix LED, the order screen's font: blocky, gridded,
small. As the thought grows, the words leave the grid. They become wide, thin, letterspaced type whose letters drift
apart while on screen, the way galaxies do (Hubble's law: farther letters drift faster). On the honk, every letter
slams back into the dot grid. Key words pop at 2x in the accent colour.

**D. Colour arc.** Sodium orange and menu-board purple at night, then indigo, then the full nebula spectrum
(magenta, teal, gold) at the peak, then a hard cut to flat, ugly window fluorescent. The last shot is night purple
again, except one gold glint in his eye: the only cosmic colour that survives the snap back.

**E. Sound.** Car idle hum, a lo-fi beat on the radio, speaker crackle. On "anything else?" the speaker's reverb tail
stretches forever, the beat filters away and a slow choir-like pad rises with the zoom. Peak: a bright chord at the
universe. Honk + record scratch, the lo-fi beat comes back exactly where it left off. Last sound: a tiny sparkle
"ting" on the glint.

## Sourcing (honest)

- Drive-thru, car, speaker, window, receipt: drawn in code. No Taco Bell logo, bell mark or brand colours on any
  surface; the name is only said and written as words. A generic purple-lit menu board.
- Neurons, stellar nursery, deep field, galaxy, eye: real photos, free licence, from Openverse (NASA / ESA / Webb /
  Flickr CC BY), credited at the end and in CREDITS.md. The cloud can't reach Pexels/NASA video, so these are stills
  with camera moves; real video would need your laptop.
- Score and effects: synthesized here (no credits).
- Voice: Higgsfield text-to-speech (~6 credits for one take of each voice). Asked before spending.
