## Every hand at once

[[quote text: You don't love poker for the cards. | highlight: cards]] You don't love poker for the cards. [[stock poker cards face down on table]] You love it for the moment right before they're turned over.
[[superposition dur: 7]] Because in that moment, your opponent's hand isn't one hand. It's every hand they could have, all at once, each with a different weight.
[[term term: Superposition | pronounce: soo-per-puh-ZISH-un | definition: A system in several possible states at once, until it is measured. | plain: In poker terms: every hand in their range is live until the cards are shown.]] Physicists have a word for that. Superposition.
[[stat value: 1,326 | label: possible two-card starting hands | sub: and a good player weighs every one]] There are one thousand three hundred and twenty six ways to be dealt two cards, and a good player weighs every single one.

## The measurement

[[collapse dur: 7 | mark.flipAt: boom]] Then the river card comes.
[[sfx hit]] Boom. The wave function collapses, and all of those possible hands become one hand.
[[quote text: Every bet you make is a measurement. | highlight: measurement]] Here's the part most players miss. Every bet you make is a measurement, and measurement changes the system.
[[steps steps: Uncertain - many hands possible; Observe - every bet is a clue; Update - re-weight the range; Collapse - the river decides]] You start uncertain. You watch. You update. And then the cards decide.
