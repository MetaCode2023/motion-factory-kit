THE PHYSICS OF POKER

— Every hand at once —

You don't love poker for the cards. You love it for the moment right before they're turned over.
Because in that moment, your opponent's hand isn't one hand. It's every hand they could have, all at once, each with a different weight.
Physicists have a word for that. Superposition.
There are one thousand three hundred and twenty six ways to be dealt two cards, and a good player weighs every single one.

— The measurement —

Then the river card comes.
Boom. The wave function collapses, and all of those possible hands become one hand.
Here's the part most players miss. Every bet you make is a measurement, and measurement changes the system.
You start uncertain. You watch. You update. And then the cards decide.

— Deception —

Which is why bluffing works.
A bluff is noise injected into the signal. It only works if your strong hands and your weak hands look exactly the same.
That's the deception. Not lying once. Making every observation look the same.
So next time you sit down at a table, remember. You're not playing cards. You're playing physics.
